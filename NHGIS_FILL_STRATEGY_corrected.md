## What NHGIS Can Fill (Priority Order)

### **HIGH PRIORITY** — Core demographic time series

1. **1990 Census data** (all demographics)
   - Population, race/ethnicity, housing tenure
   - Completes your 1990–2023 demographic timeline (currently starts at 2000)
   - Need tract-level for **2010 tract definitions** (NHGIS can harmonize via crosswalk files)
   - Critical for the Compare View time series (§5 in DATA_METHODOLOGY) which charts DVI and demographics from 1990 onward — currently those pre-2000 data points are empty or extrapolated

2. **2000 decennial data for median age, education, foreign-born, 65+, rent burden**
   - **⚡CORRECTED priority:** These five ACS-derived fields are completely missing at year 2000 in the demographics file. The Decennial Census 2000 SF1 (your current source for 2000) did not include them. NHGIS can provide 2000 values via the Census 2000 Summary File 3 (SF3), which was the long-form sample that collected age distributions, educational attainment, and nativity.
   - This single pull would raise `median_age`, `pct_bachelors_degree_or_higher`, and `pct_foreign_born` from 63.5% → ~79% coverage, and `pct_65_and_over` from 45% → ~61%.

3. **1990–2005 Property baseline** (housing & values)
   - Total housing units, vacancy rate, homeownership
   - Median home values (available in 1990/2000 Census long form)
   - Would need to interpolate for 1995, 2005

4. **1990–2005 Socioeconomic baseline** (income & poverty)
   - Median household income (available in Decennial Census 2000 SF3; limited in 1990)
   - Poverty rate (available in Decennial Census 2000 SF3)

### **MEDIUM PRIORITY** — Fill specific field gaps

5. **⚡CORRECTED: pct_65_and_over at 2010 and 2015**
   - Currently 0% at 2000/2005/2010 and 61.7% at 2015. The 2010 gap is fillable from the ACS 2006–2010 five-year estimates (same source that provides your 2010 median_age data). The partial 2015 gap (98 missing tracts) may come from tracts that were too small for reliable ACS age breakdowns.

6. **⚡CORRECTED: median_age, education, foreign-born at 2005 and 2010**
   - These are only 36.2% (2005) and 38.3% (2010) complete. The 2005 values are interpolated from 2000/2010 — but since 2000 itself has no data for these fields, the interpolation is unreliable for ~160 tracts. Filling 2000 from NHGIS (priority #2 above) would automatically improve the 2005 interpolations.
   - The 2010 gap (~62% missing) is surprising given that ACS 2006–2010 should cover all tracts. Worth investigating whether `fill_census_gaps_v2.py` queried the right ACS tables for these fields.

7. **⚡CORRECTED: unemployment_rate at 2010**
   - 100% null at 2010 but ~99.6% complete at 2015/2020/2023. NHGIS can fill 2010 from ACS 2006–2010 (table B23025 — Employment Status).

8. **⚡ADDED: rent_burden_pct at 2000 and partial 2005/2010**
   - This field feeds directly into the DVI Demographic Vulnerability sub-index (50% weight per §3.1 of DATA_METHODOLOGY). It's missing at 2000 entirely and only ~35–37% present at 2005/2010. NHGIS can provide 2000 values from Census 2000 SF3 (gross rent as percentage of household income). The 2005 interpolation would improve once 2000 anchors exist.

### **LOW PRIORITY** — Minor field completeness

9. Fill sparse nulls in already-well-covered fields:
   - `pct_owner_occupied` (7 nulls across all years)
   - `median_household_income` (14 nulls, ~1.3%)
   - `poverty_rate` (4 nulls, ~0.4%)
   - `gini_coefficient` (11 nulls, ~1%)
   - These are likely tracts with very small populations where ACS suppressed estimates for statistical reliability. Consider whether interpolation from adjacent years is preferable to pulling from NHGIS (which would return the same suppressed values).

10. **⚡ADDED: snap_participation_rate at 2010/2015**
    - 61.5% coverage at both years. SNAP data is harder to get from NHGIS — the ACS table (B22003) may not cover all tracts at this geography. Consider whether the Texas HHS administrative data (already listed as a source in DATA_METHODOLOGY §11) can fill these gaps instead.

## What NHGIS CANNOT Fill

- **Eviction filing rates** — NHGIS doesn't have this; need BASTA Austin or Eviction Lab
- **Dominant industries** — NHGIS doesn't aggregate to this; would need Census Bureau's LEHD/OnTheMap or BLS QCEW
- **Uninsured percentage** — Very sparse in NHGIS; limited to modern ACS. Currently only 61.5% at 2015 in your data. The ACS table (B27001) exists but has high margins of error at tract level.
- **Property tax** — NHGIS doesn't have; need TCAD (Travis Central Appraisal District). Per DATA_METHODOLOGY §11, TCAD is already a listed source — this may be a pipeline issue rather than a source issue.
- **Commercial sqft** — NHGIS doesn't track; already sourced from City of Austin permits
- **Home value year-over-year change** — Derived field, not source data. Can be computed once `median_home_value` gaps are filled.
- **Rent burden** at 2005 — Not directly available from NHGIS for intercensal years. Must be interpolated from 2000 (Census SF3) and 2010 (ACS) anchors.

## Recommended Data Pull from NHGIS

### Extract 1: 1990 Decennial Census (complete baseline)
**Dataset:** `1990_STF3` (Summary Tape File 3 — sample data with socioeconomic variables)
**Geographic level:** Census tract
**Geographic extent:** Travis County (FIPS 48453)
**Tables to request:**
- P001: Total population
- P006/P008: Race/ethnicity (White, Black, Hispanic origin, Asian/Pacific Islander)
- H003/H004: Housing tenure (owner-occupied, renter-occupied)
- P080A: Median household income
- P117: Poverty status
- P013A: Median age
- P057: Educational attainment (bachelor's degree or higher)
- P037: Foreign-born population
- P012: Age distribution (for 65+ calculation)
- H043: Gross rent as percentage of household income (for rent burden)

**Note:** Use `1990_STF3` not `1990_STF1`. STF1 is the 100% count but only has population, race, and housing tenure. STF3 has the sample-based socioeconomic variables you need (income, education, age, nativity, rent burden).

### Extract 2: 2000 Decennial Census SF3 (fill ACS-type gaps)
**Dataset:** `2000_SF3`
**Geographic level:** Census tract
**Geographic extent:** Travis County (FIPS 48453)
**Tables to request:**
- P037: Median age
- P025: Educational attainment (bachelor's or higher)
- P021: Nativity (foreign-born)
- P012: Age by sex (for 65+ calculation)
- H069: Gross rent as percentage of household income
- P053: Median household income (for crosscheck against existing 2000 data)
- P087: Poverty status (for crosscheck)

**Purpose:** Your existing 2000 data comes from SF1 which lacks these fields. SF3 adds them. Cross-check population and race/ethnicity against your existing SF1 values (should match within rounding).

### Extract 3: ACS 2006–2010 (fill 2010 gaps)
**Dataset:** `2006_2010_ACS5a`
**Geographic level:** Census tract
**Geographic extent:** Travis County

- B01002: Median age
- B15003: Educational attainment

- B25070: Gross rent as percentage of household income

**Purpose:** Fill the ~62% gap in `median_age`, `pct_bachelors_degree_or_higher`, `pct_foreign_born` at year 2010, plus the 100% gap in `unemployment_rate` at 2010 and the 100% gap in `pct_65_and_over` at 2010.

## Data Validation Strategy

### Recommended: Option 1 — Spot-Check Against Known Census Values

Since you already have 2000 Census SF1 data in your demographics JSON, use it as a validator:

1. Pull 2000 SF3 from NHGIS for Travis County tracts
2. Compare against your existing 2000 values for fields that exist in both SF1 and SF3:
   - Total population (should match exactly)
   - Race/ethnicity percentages (should match within rounding)
   - Owner occupancy % (should match within ±1%)
3. If values match within ±2%, the NHGIS data is aligned with your existing pipeline
4. Then use the same NHGIS extract to fill the SF3-only fields (median age, education, nativity, rent burden) that are currently missing at 2000
5. Proceed to 1990 pull with confidence

**Additional validation:** Compare the NHGIS ACS 2006–2010 pull against your existing 2010 data for `median_age` (38.3% present). The 98 tracts that already have 2010 median_age values should match the NHGIS values within the ACS margin of error.

### Option 2: Accept NHGIS as Authoritative

NHGIS is the standard source for historical Census data. It's maintained by the University of Minnesota, peer-reviewed, and used in academic research. Your DATA_METHODOLOGY already lists U.S. Census Bureau as the primary source — NHGIS is just a more convenient access point for the same underlying data.

**Advantage:** Saves validation work, harmonized tract boundaries already handled, transparent documentation.

**Recommendation:** Start with Option 1 for the 2000 crosscheck, then proceed with Option 2 for 1990 (where you have no existing data to validate against).

## DVI Impact Assessment

Filling these gaps will materially improve the Displacement Vulnerability Index calculations described in DATA_METHODOLOGY §3:

**Demographic Vulnerability sub-index (35% of DVI):**
- `rent_burden_pct` carries 50% weight in this sub-index but is currently missing at 2000 and ~65% missing at 2005/2010. Filling 2000 from Census SF3 and re-interpolating 2005 would give the DVI meaningful pre-2015 demographic vulnerability scores instead of relying on re-weighting (§3.2).
- `pct_foreign_born` carries 20% weight and has the same gap pattern. Filling 2000 fixes the anchor for 2005 interpolation.

**Socioeconomic Stress sub-index (30% of DVI):**
- `unemployment_rate` carries 30% weight but is 100% null at 2010. This means the entire 2010 socioeconomic stress sub-index is computed without unemployment — a significant gap given that 2010 was the peak of the Great Recession's labor market impact. Filling from ACS 2006–2010 is high-value.

**Trajectory Lens (§6.1):**
- Velocity is computed as `(DVI_2023 - DVI_2010) / 13`. If the 2010 DVI is systematically under- or over-estimated due to missing unemployment and rent burden data, all velocity and acceleration calculations are affected.

**Compare View (§5):**
- DVI time series charts 8 snap years including 1990, 1995, 2000, and 2005. Without 1990 data, the first four points are either missing or extrapolated with low confidence. The 1990 NHGIS pull would give these charts a real baseline.
